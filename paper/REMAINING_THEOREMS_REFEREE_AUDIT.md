## 1. Theorem 3: complete-core signatures

Let U induce a complete reflexive relation and S be its complement. For each J subset S suppose there is u_J in U with outgoing S-neighborhood J and incoming arrows from all S. If S is empty, a deterministic balanced partition has all required row hits with failure probability O(m rho^(floor(m/|U|))), exponentially small.

Otherwise set s=|S| and reserve W of floor(m/2) vertices. Partition as much of W as possible into disjoint ordered s-tuples and examine the s-by-s internal edge coordinates for each tuple, including loops. A tuple matches the entire induced S relation with fixed probability theta^e rho^(s^2-e)>0. The tuple tests are independent. Failure of every test has probability exp(-Omega(m)). Select the first successful tuple, using only the tested coordinates internal to W. Assign its distinct vertices to the singleton S fibres.

For a nonselected vertex x, define J(x) as the exact set of S singleton vertices it hits and I(x) as the exact set of singleton vertices hitting it. Outside W, all 2s tested coordinates per x are fresh, and the signature pairs are independent across x, conditional on the tuple history. Each of the 4^s signatures has fixed positive probability at least min(theta,rho)^(2s). Chernoff and a union bound give at least delta*m vertices of each signature outside W except with exponentially small probability, for a fixed delta>0. For each u in U reserve a disjoint group of at least floor(delta*m/(2|U|)) such outside-W vertices with the exact signatures J_u,I_u required by u. This remains possible when multiple labels require identical signatures. Assign every leftover nonselected vertex x to u_{J(x)}; it has the correct outgoing signature and permits every incoming signature, so forth is satisfied for S-to-U edges. The reserved exact groups guarantee back for every required S-to-U hit. Nonselected vertices inside W may have signatures correlated with tuple tests, but no distributional claim about their signatures is used.

After exposing the signatures and the tuple tests, all coordinates from nonselected rows ending outside W are unexposed: tuple tests were internal to W, and the signature tests have a selected singleton as an endpoint. In particular all nonselected rows have independent original Bernoulli edges to the disjoint reserved U groups outside W, conditional on the complete exposure history and the deterministic assignments. Each such group has linear size. Union bound over at most m|U| required hits gives exp(-Omega(m)) failure. The S-to-S edges were fixed by the tuple selection, U-to-S by exact outgoing signatures, S-to-U by incoming compatibility and the reserved groups, U-to-U by completeness and the new hit tests. This yields a full surjective bounded morphism with exponentially high probability. Absorbing finite prefactors into the exponential constant proves Theorem 3. No assumption of independent signatures inside the reserve was required.

Actual finite check: for all 104 canonical four-point central targets, tested all 15 nonempty subsets U (1,560 candidates). The predicate holds for exactly the nine encodings 001011011, 001111011, 011011011, 011011111, 101011011, 101110111, 101111101, 101111111, 111111111, corresponding to 43 labelled peripheral matrices. For every successful partition, checked every possible leftover signature pair against the selected representative (33 checks). No zero-limit type met the predicate.

## 2. Forced-negative targets 001011101 and 001101011

The peripheral missing-row signatures in Section 5.1 are correct after the indicated relabellings. Both targets force the independent B fibre, with A precisely the outside-B rows missing B. No universally incoming target label misses exactly B; hence the label-specific tiny-fibre argument in Theorem 2 excludes b<L-3log_B L up to exponentially small error. Independent B, and independent A in the second case, have size <ceil(128L) outside the empty-rectangle exception. All exceptional errors decay faster than the claimed polynomial bounds.

For fixed independent B, expose its columns. Each outside row independently misses B with probability q_b=rho^b, and the resulting A is measurable in this history. The coordinates B x A and A x A are fresh conditional on this exposure (for each fixed outcome A outside B). For the first target the only further required condition retained is absence of B x A. Its conditional probability q_b^a gives E[q_b^a]=(1-q_b+q_b^2)^(m-b); this upper count even allows A empty. With lambda=m q_b, b=O(L), q_b<=L^3/m, the first moment at size b is <= exp(O(L^6/m)) pi_lambda(b), from the factorial inequality and ln(1-x)<=-x. The normalized mean lambda/b decreases by factor <rho at each increment. Only a fixed number of integers lie in [1/2,2]; their Poisson masses are O(L^-1/2). The other O(L) integers contribute exp(-Omega(L)) by the positive Poisson rate function. This gives O(L^-1/2).

For the second target the further conditions retained are absence of A x A and that every B row hits A, in disjoint fresh blocks. For a>=1 the count at (a,b) is exactly the product of the two binomial coefficients, rho^(b^2), q_b^a (1-q_b)^(m-b-a), rho^(a^2), and (1-rho^a)^b. It is an upper count for successful maps, not a count of full maps. Bounding factorials and hit factors yields the product

    exp(O(L^4/m)) pi_lambda(b) * t^a pi_(b rho^a)(a),   t=lambda/b.

The uniform correction follows because (a+b)q_b=O(L^4/m). The large-a truncation a>8log_B L uses lambda<=L^3 and (lambda rho^a)^a/a!<=B^(-a^2/2) on that range. Summing O(L^2) terms of this superpolynomially small order is negligible. For a<=8log_B L the second factor is at most exp(O((ln L)^2)); if |t-1|>L^-1/4, the Poisson rate for b is >=c L^-1/2, giving exp(-Omega(sqrt L)), dominating that error. For |t-1|<=L^-1/4 there is at most one integer b for large m, as the ratio <rho is fixed. Then t^a<=2 and pi_lambda(b)=O(L^-1/2). The sum over a>=1 of pi_(b rho^a)(a) is O((log b)^-1/2): with H=log_B b, indices a<=H/2 have mean >=sqrt b and total exponentially small; for a>H/2 the normalized means decrease geometrically, so only O(1) indices in [1/2,2] contribute O(H^-1/2), while all others have a geometric tail exp(-c a). This gives O((L log L)^-1/2). No matching lower bound of polynomial order is claimed.

## 3. Dominating-reserve star

For the fixed k-arrow missing star, let W have size floor(m/2), t=ceil(L-1.5log_B L), q=rho^t. The count X of t-subsets D of W hit by every row has E[X]>0. For two sets with intersection j, the normalized joint probability is (1+(rho^(2t-j)-q^2)/(1-q)^2)^m. With j<=t/2 this is <=1+o(1) uniformly because m rho^(3t/2)<=m^-1/2 L^(9/4). With j>t/2 it is <=exp(4L^(3/2)); the overlap probability is exp(-Omega(L^2)) by the union bound over subsets of size ceil(t/2) of the first set. Thus E[X^2]/E[X]^2<=1+o(1), and Cauchy-Schwarz gives existence a.a.s. Choose D deterministically using only edges ending in W.

Expose D-row edges ending outside W, fresh given the first history. Their common nonneighbor set outside W has independent column indicators with probability q; its mean is Theta(L^(3/2)). Hence it contains k disjoint sets of size ceil(2L) a.a.s., all of which the D rows miss. Assign them to the k peripheral destinations and the remaining vertices outside D to the center. Non-D-row edges ending outside W remain fresh given both histories, so each non-D row hits each of these fibres and the linear-size outside-W center part except with probability O(k/m)+exp(-Omega(m)). D rows hit the center if they have any edge outside W, since all the k chosen fibres are missed by them. Before the second exposure their probability of failing this is at most t rho^(m-|W|)=o(1); a union bound with the other events is sufficient and does not assert fresh bits conditional on the final selection. All rows hit D by construction. The exact successor equalities follow. The proof gives convergence to one, not an exponential failure rate.

## 4. Theorem 6 and ordinary characteristic formulas

In the universal-modality language, a countervaluation to chi_F=not [U] Delta_F makes Delta_F true at every source point. The existential label conjuncts ensure every label occurs, the pointwise disjunction and disjointness conjuncts ensure exactly one label per point, the diamond conditions ensure required successor hits, and the box conditions exclude all forbidden successors. Hence such a countervaluation induces a full surjective bounded morphism; conversely pull back the singleton label valuation. The equivalence is global, not a generated-subframe condition.

On sources with universal R^2, [U]=box box and its dual=diamond diamond by structural induction, including at the root and inside Delta_F. For every ordered pair x,y, the m-2 intermediates distinct from both endpoints provide independent two-edge tests of probability theta^2 (for x=y there are actually m-1 such intermediates). The tests use distinct ordered coordinates even for x=y. Union bound gives probability of nonuniversal R^2 <=m^2(1-theta^2)^(m-2). In particular the difference between ordinary characteristic-formula validity probability and 1-p_m(F) tends to zero.

For finite central F, if limsup p_m(F)>0, any formula invalid in F is invalid in every source admitting the full bounded morphism, since a falsifying valuation pulls back along it. Therefore that formula cannot have frame-validity probability tending to one. Conversely if limsup p_m(F)=0, p_m(F)->0; the ordinary characteristic formula is almost surely valid but fails in F itself, using identity and centrality (R^2 universal). This proves the exact finite-model criterion without assuming convergence of p_m(F). Normal modal inference preserves almost-sure validity: modus ponens uses a union bound, necessitation and substitution preserve frame validity deterministically. Thus the almost-sure logic is normal.

Every fixed finite extension axiom of the countable random relation holds a.a.s. at fixed bias: outside any tuple of k vertices a candidate realizes the prescribed 2k directed bits and loop with probability at least min(theta,rho)^(2k+1), and candidates are independent. A polynomial union bound over tuples and the finitely many patterns yields the result. The passage from these extension axioms to ML^r, and its finite-model characterization by central frames, uses Goranko 2020 Propositions 3.2(ii) and 3.5, as recorded in the manuscript. The finite-model equivalence is an adaptation of his Proposition 4.2(ii), not claimed as new. Re-examining all central target and source relations of size at most three in actual JS execution: 77 source relations have universal R^2, 453 do not. On the universal ones, 1,397 source/target pairs and 608,054 independent propositional valuations were checked. Evaluated the ordinary translated formula by truth-set operations (two diamond and two box applications) and compared countervalidity with direct surjective-map enumeration. All equivalences passed. The small cases are corroboration, not the general proof.

## 5. Minimality, independence and prime central cycles

For distinct minimal vanishing central types F,H at the specified bias, no full bounded morphism F->H exists: if smaller by minimality, if larger by cardinality, if equal size a bijective bounded morphism is an isomorphism. Centrality gives universal R^2 and F validates ML^r, so it validates every other translated characteristic formula but not its own. This establishes independence of the displayed finite family (25 or 26 schemes according to the largest threshold) by ordinary frame soundness. It does NOT establish completeness or rule out arbitrary finite axiomatizations.

For C_r, a peripheral i has successor set {c,i+1}. The image z of c is universal both ways. If some peripheral maps to z, successor-image equality forces at most two target labels. With two labels and the other label looped the row equality forces all peripherals onto the other label, contradictory. With the other loop absent, equality forces alternating colours and r even. If no peripheral maps to z, each other target label has a unique successor besides z. Surjectivity and the periodic source cycle imply that these successors form one cycle of length d dividing r. Conversely reduction modulo d, the alternating even colouring, and the constant singleton map all work. Thus the quotient classification follows. With q=r^2-r and incidence degree 2(r-1), H1/H2 hold for r>=2. Theorem 2 implies vanishing, including critical r=2. Among r>=2 minimal vanishing cycles are precisely odd primes. They yield infinitely many independent characteristic schemes over ML^r, not a proof of non-finite axiomatizability. No first-discovery claim; Goranko has a known independent path family.

Actual JS enumeration of all set partitions of r+1 vertices for r=2..7 (5,15,52,203,877,4140; total 5292) identified the quotient relation whenever successor images are constant on each partition block. All and only the listed types appeared; the number of valid partitions respectively 5,3,6,3,7,3. Each divisor-cycle quotient occurred once, the singleton once, and the even alternating quotient twice (choice of peripheral parity merging into the central fibre). This is an independent combinatorial check, not a study of the five-point random-frame census.
