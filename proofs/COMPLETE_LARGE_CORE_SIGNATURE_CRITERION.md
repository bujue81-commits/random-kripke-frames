# Complete-large-core signature criterion

Internal hand proof, not Lean-verified, independently refereed or novelty-checked. This strictly generalizes UNIVERSAL_COLUMN_SIGNATURE_CRITERION.md.

## Theorem

Fix a nonempty finite directed target F, with loops allowed, and independent ordered source edges (including loops) of fixed probability theta in (0,1). Partition its labels into a nonempty large core U and a possibly empty set S. Assume:

(H1) U induces a complete reflexive directed graph.
(H2) For each J subset of S there is a label u_J in U whose outgoing successor set in S equals J and which receives arrows from every label in S.

Then for some c>0 and all sufficiently large m,

    P(no surjective bounded morphism G_m -> F) <= exp(-c*m).

The representatives u_J have universal incoming columns because of H1. Unlike the earlier criterion, other labels in U need not have universal incoming columns. The induced relation on S is unrestricted.

## Proof

If S is empty use a deterministic balanced partition into |U| parts: failure of a required row hit has probability <=m*|U|*(1-theta)^floor(m/|U|), and forth is automatic.

Otherwise set s=|S|, rho=1-theta. Reserve a deterministic W of floor(m/2) source vertices, divided into disjoint ordered s-tuples. Choose the first tuple whose internal ordered edges exactly realize the induced relation on S. Independent tuple tests have fixed positive success probability theta^e*rho^(s^2-e) where e is the number of internal arrows of S, so failure is exp(-Omega(m)). Use the selected vertices v_j, one per j in S, as singleton fibres. All selection tests are inside W.

For each x outside the selected tuple, expose two signatures:

    J(x)={j in S : x->v_j},
    I(x)={j in S : v_j->x}.

For u in U put J_u={j in S : u->j} and I_u={j in S : j->u}. A vertex with J(x)=J_u and I(x) subset I_u can be assigned to u: outgoing edges to singleton fibres match exactly, and no forbidden incoming edge from a singleton occurs. Furthermore vertices with the exact pair (J_u,I_u) ensure every required incoming singleton arrow is witnessed.

Conditional on the full tuple-selection history, the signatures of vertices outside W are mutually independent. The pair (J,I) has probability theta^(|J|+|I|)*rho^(2s-|J|-|I|), strictly positive, because the coordinates in both directions between distinct outside vertices and the selected tuple are fresh, disjoint ordered pairs. By Chernoff and a union bound over 4^s patterns, all signature pairs occur linearly many times outside W with probability 1-exp(-Omega(m)).

For each u in U, allocate a disjoint linear-size group of outside-W vertices of exact signature (J_u,I_u) to its fibre. This is possible even when several u share a pair: divide its available vertices into enough groups, each of size at least alpha*m for a fixed alpha>0. Assign every still-unallocated vertex x outside the selected tuple to u_{J(x)} from H2; it accepts every possible incoming signature since I_{u_J}=S. This determines a partition with all U-fibres large outside W, each containing vertices witnessing all its required incoming arrows from S.

For conditional independence of the remaining required edges, take the sigma-field generated by all coordinates with at least one endpoint in W. It contains all selection and signature information, all allocations and their size event. The edges with both endpoints outside W are independent of this sigma-field. Thus for every row x outside W, conditional failure to hit the outside-W portion of any U-fibre is <=rho^(alpha*m). This alone does not check rows inside W, since all edges from W are in this sigma-field. Instead use the finer exposure history: expose only tuple tests inside W, and all signature coordinates between the selected tuple and other vertices. Conditional on the selected tuple and these tests, the ordered edges x->y for x not in the selected tuple, y outside W remain fresh. Indeed the tuple-selection history uses only coordinates in W x W, and for fixed selected tuple the signature tests have at least one endpoint in that tuple. Their coordinate sets are disjoint from the stated fresh coordinates. The allocations can be chosen deterministically from precisely that history. Therefore every row not in the selected tuple (including rows in W minus the tuple) hits every U-fibre except with conditional probability <=m*|U|*rho^(alpha*m). For selected rows v_j, the exact-signature groups already guarantee each permitted hit into U, and forbid every nonpermitted one. The induced-tuple test handles all edges between singleton S-fibres. All U-to-U arrows are permitted by H1. These checks give the full successor-image equality and surjectivity. Combining exponential errors proves the theorem.

## Four-point application

The previous census had 9 unresolved canonical peripheral matrices. Exactly four satisfy the new criterion, with the following singleton small label (peripheral labels are 1,2,3 in matrix order):

    001011011  S={1}
    001111011  S={1}
    101011011  S={1}
    101110111  S={2}

For each, U is the center and the other two peripheral labels; U is complete reflexive. Among U labels, the center realizes the hit signature on S and another universal-incoming label realizes the miss signature. The remaining large label may have missing incoming arrows from S; exact-pair groups enforce those constraints.

Thus these four have p_m >=1-exp(-c*m). They cannot be minimal vanishing-probability targets. The other five unresolved types fail the criterion (which is not evidence for vanishing):

    001011101,001101011,011010111,011011101,100111111.

The updated census has 90 zero-limit, nine one-limit, five unresolved among 104 isomorphism types. At least 20 minimal four-point types remain established by preceding hand proofs and quotient enumeration. The new theorem also covers the earlier positive criterion and has exponential failure bounds. No claim about necessity, all four-point limits, or general almost-sure logic. No new Lean source or independent literature-priority verification.
